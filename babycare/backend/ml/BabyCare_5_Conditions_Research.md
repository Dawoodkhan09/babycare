## 1. FEVER (High body temperature)

**Definition.** Fever = body temperature **≥ 38 °C (100.4 °F)**. It is a sign, not a disease — usually the body fighting an infection. [NHS]

**Why it happens (causes).**
- **Viral infections** (most common) — colds, flu, viral rashes.
- **Bacterial infections** — ear infection, throat infection, urine infection, pneumonia.
- Post-**vaccination** (mild, short-lived), overheating, teething (only a *slight* rise — high fever should not be blamed on teething). [NHS]

**Types / grades.**
- Low-grade: 38–38.9 °C | High-grade: ≥ 39 °C.
- By cause: viral vs bacterial. Pattern: continuous, intermittent, or recurrent.

**Key symptoms.** Hot forehead/body, flushed face, sweating or shivering, irritability, tiredness, poor feeding, sometimes chills.

**Organic / home care (safe, evidence-based).**
- Offer plenty of **fluids** (frequent small sips); keep **breastfeeding**.
- **Light clothing**, do not over-wrap; keep room comfortably cool (no cold fanning).
- **Paracetamol** or **ibuprofen** at the correct weight/age dose *if the child is uncomfortable*. **Never aspirin** in children (Reye's syndrome).
- Rest. [NHS]

**Homeopathic (traditional reference — Boericke's *Materia Medica*; not proven):**
- **Aconite** — sudden fever after exposure to cold/dry wind; restless, anxious, dry heat.
- **Belladonna** — high fever with red hot face, throbbing, dilated pupils.
- **Ferrum phosphoricum** — early, low-grade fever, no clear localisation.
- **Gelsemium** — flu-like fever, drowsy, dull, aching, chills up the spine.

**🚑 Emergency / see a doctor now if:**
- **Any fever in a baby under 3 months (≥ 38 °C)** → urgent.
- Fever in 3–6 months ≥ 39 °C; fever lasting **> 5 days**.
- Stiff neck, light hurts eyes, severe headache; **rash that looks like bruises**.
- Difficulty breathing; drowsy/hard to wake; fits (seizure).
- Signs of dehydration (few wet nappies, < half usual fluids). [NHS]

*Sources:* NHS – Fever in children (North Bristol NHS Trust); standard pediatric thresholds (NICE/AAP).

## 2. COLD & COUGH (Runny nose, sneezing)

**Definition.** The common cold is a **viral** upper-respiratory infection. Children get 6–8+ colds a year; most settle in **7–10 days** (cough can linger 2–3 weeks). [healthdirect / WebMD]

**Why it happens (causes).** Viruses (rhinovirus, RSV and others), spread by droplets and touch. Not caused by cold weather itself. [healthdirect]

**Types.** Common cold (viral URI); may overlap with flu (more fever/body-ache) or progress to chest cough/bronchiolitis.

**Key symptoms.** Runny/blocked nose, sneezing, cough, sore throat, mild fever, watery eyes, reduced appetite, fussiness.

**Organic / home care (safe, evidence-based).**
- **Fluids** and rest; keep breastfeeding.
- **Honey** for cough in children **over 1 year** (≈1 teaspoon) — as effective as some OTC cough syrups in trials. **NEVER give honey under 1 year** (risk of infant **botulism**). [WebMD]
- **Saline nose drops + gentle suction** for a blocked nose; **cool-mist humidifier**; warm fluids to soothe throat.
- **Do NOT give OTC cough/cold medicines to young children** (no proven benefit; cough syrups not for under-4). [WebMD]

**Homeopathic (traditional reference — Boericke; not proven):**
- **Allium cepa** — streaming watery nose, burning; worse warm room.
- **Aconite** — very early, sudden onset after cold wind.
- **Bryonia** — dry, painful cough, worse with movement.
- **Pulsatilla** — thick yellow mucus, clingy/weepy child, better in open air.
- **Spongia / Hepar sulph** — barking/croupy cough.

**🚑 Emergency / see a doctor if:** trouble breathing, bluish lips, stridor (high-pitched breathing), persistent ear pain, high fever, cough > 3 weeks, signs of dehydration, or a baby under 3 months with a cold. [WebMD]

*Sources:* healthdirect (Australia) – Coughs & colds in children; WebMD – Remedies for toddler cough; AKU randomised honey-vs-cough study.

## 3. TEETHING PAIN (Gum irritation, drooling)

**Definition.** Normal eruption of baby teeth, usually **6–24 months**. Causes mild discomfort — **not** high fever or illness.

**Why it happens.** Teeth pushing through the gums causes local inflammation.

**Signs (not a disease).** Drooling, gum irritation/swelling, chewing/biting objects, mild irritability, slightly raised temperature (**not** a true high fever), disturbed sleep, cheek rubbing.
> If a baby has **high fever, diarrhoea or looks unwell, do not blame teething** — look for another cause.

**Organic / safe care (recommended):**
- **Chilled (not frozen) teething ring**; clean **cold washcloth** to chew.
- **Gently rub the gums** with a clean finger.
- Extra comfort/cuddling; wipe drool to prevent rash.
- Ask a pediatrician before any medicine.

**⚠️ Homeopathic — SAFETY WARNING:**
- The **FDA warned parents NOT to use homeopathic teething tablets/gels**. Testing found **inconsistent, sometimes dangerous amounts of belladonna** (a toxin). The FDA investigated **seizures and the deaths of at least 10 babies**. **Do not use these products.** [FDA via Providence Health]
- The traditional homeopathic text lists **Chamomilla** for a very irritable teething child (one cheek red, wants to be carried) and **Calcarea phosphorica** — but these must **never** be self-administered to a baby; consult a professional. Safe physical measures above are preferred.

**🚑 See a doctor if:** high fever, diarrhoea, poor feeding, or the baby seems genuinely unwell (these are **not** teething).

*Sources:* FDA homeopathic teething warning (reported via Providence Health, 2017); NIH/FDA statements on homeopathy; standard pediatric (AAP) teething guidance.

## 4. DIARRHEA (Loose or watery stools)

**Definition.** Passing **3+ loose/watery stools in 24 hours**. The main danger is **dehydration**. Diarrhoea is a leading cause of child death worldwide — but is easily treatable. [WHO/UNICEF]

**Why it happens (causes).** Usually **infection**: **rotavirus** (leading viral cause), other viruses, **bacteria** (E. coli, Shigella, Salmonella), parasites; contaminated food/water; sometimes antibiotics or food intolerance. [WHO]

**Types.**
- **Acute watery** (hours–days, e.g. rotavirus/cholera),
- **Bloody / dysentery** (with blood — bacterial),
- **Persistent** (≥ 14 days).

**Key symptoms.** Frequent loose stools, tummy cramps, vomiting, mild fever, reduced appetite; **watch for dehydration** — dry mouth, few/no wet nappies, sunken eyes, no tears, lethargy.

**Organic / treatment (WHO/UNICEF — evidence-based, first-line):**
- **Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)** — low-osmolarity — after each loose stool to replace fluids/salts. Keep **breastfeeding/feeding**.
- **Zinc supplementation**: **20 mg/day for 10–14 days** (**10 mg/day for infants under 6 months**) — shortens illness and prevents recurrence.
- Continue normal feeding; good hygiene/handwashing; **rotavirus vaccine** prevents it.
- Avoid anti-diarrhoeal drugs in young children unless a doctor advises. [WHO/UNICEF]

**Homeopathic (traditional reference — Boericke; not proven, never a substitute for ORS):**
- **Arsenicum album** — diarrhoea + vomiting after bad food; restless, exhausted.
- **Podophyllum** — profuse, gushing, watery stool.
- **Chamomilla** — greenish stool during teething, very irritable.
- **China (Cinchona)** — weakness/exhaustion from fluid loss.

**🚑 Emergency / see a doctor now if:** signs of **dehydration**, **blood in stool**, persistent vomiting (can't keep fluids down), diarrhoea **> 14 days**, high fever, or **any baby under 1 year with watery diarrhoea**. ORS is still given on the way.

*Sources:* WHO/UNICEF Joint Statement on the Clinical Management of Acute Diarrhoea (ORS + zinc); WHO diarrhoeal disease guidance; NCBI review (PMC3045093).

## 5. SLEEP ISSUES (Restlessness at night)

**Definition.** Difficulty falling asleep or frequent night waking. Night waking is **developmentally normal** in babies/toddlers — a concern mainly when frequent, prolonged, or affecting the family. [Huckleberry]

**Why it happens (causes / types).**
- **Sleep associations** — needing a parent/feed/pacifier to fall back asleep (most common in toddlers).
- **Physical:** hunger, **teething**, illness (cold, ear infection, allergy), reflux.
- **Overtiredness / nap problems**; irregular schedule.
- **Fears & nightmares** (older toddlers); night terrors. [Huckleberry]

**Key signs.** Frequent night waking, restlessness, bedtime resistance, crying at night, early rising, daytime crankiness.

**Organic / safe approach (sleep hygiene — recommended):**
- **Calm, predictable bedtime routine** (~30–45 min: bath, pajamas, story), **no screens 1–2 h before bed**.
- Age-appropriate total sleep (**toddlers ~11–14 h/24 h**); consistent bedtime + regular nap.
- Encourage **self-soothing**; white noise / comfort object; brief, calm reassurance at night.
- Rule out hunger, teething, illness, wet nappy, too hot/cold. [Huckleberry]

**Homeopathic (traditional reference — Boericke; not proven):**
- **Chamomilla** — irritable, restless (often teething), wants to be carried.
- **Coffea cruda** — wakeful, overactive mind, excitement.
- **Cina** — restless sleep, grinding teeth (traditionally linked to worms).
- **Passiflora** — restlessness/insomnia.

**🚑 See a doctor if:** sleep problems come with **snoring/pauses in breathing**, poor growth, extreme daytime sleepiness, or an underlying illness (fever, pain, ear-pulling) — these need assessment.

*Sources:* Huckleberry (pediatric sleep) – toddler night waking; AAP sleep duration guidance; Boericke's *Materia Medica* (traditional homeopathic indications).
